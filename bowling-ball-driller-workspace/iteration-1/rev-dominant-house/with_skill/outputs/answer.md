## Read
At 16 mph a "matched" rev rate is about 250 rpm. You're at 430, a ratio of about **1.7, which puts you strongly rev-dominant**. You have far more revs than speed, so the ball has plenty of time to grab. Your low tilt (9°) and 5 1/2" PAP add to that: you roll a big track that reads the front of the lane early. Put a strong asym on a house shot with that release and you get exactly what you described. The ball burns energy in the front and mid-lane and has nothing left at the pins. Your layout needs to **save energy**, which means pushing the read down the lane and smoothing out the transition. It should not add flare.

## Target motion
Get through the front cleaner and start the turn later, with a smooth arc into the pocket instead of an early hook that rolls out.

## Recommended layout
- **Dual Angle: 70° x 5 1/2" x 55°**
- **VLS / Pin Buffer: 5 1/2" x 5 1/8" x 3 7/8"** (pin-to-PAP x PSA-to-PAP x pin buffer, using your 6" pin-to-PSA)
- **Where it lands:** pin up, roughly 5 3/8" above the midline and about 1 5/8" right of grip center (above your fingers, toward the PAP side). The MB/PSA ends up about 5" from your PAP, well away from your axis. These positions are approximate, and your driller will mark the exact spots with a layout tool.
- **Why:**
  - **5 1/2" pin-to-PAP**: puts the pin well past the max-flare zone (3 3/8"–4"). The core starts farther from its stable axis, so the ball revs up later and flares less. This is the "long pin" control family, and it's the right one when the problem is the ball using its energy too early on the fronts.
  - **70° drilling angle / ~5" PSA-to-PAP**: moving the PSA away from your PAP holds onto your axis rotation longer. You get more length before it turns over.
  - **55° VAL / ~3 7/8" buffer**: the long buffer slows the rev-up and smooths the breakpoint. High-rev players usually want this so the ball doesn't over-react when it finally sees friction.
  - The usual starting point for a ~16 mph / 420 rpm player is about 65° x 5 1/2" x 55°. I moved one step longer than that because the ball is a strong asym, your low tilt reads the lane early, and your last ball rolled out.

## If it's not quite right
Change surface first. You can't un-drill a ball.
- **Still too early / rolls out:** take the surface up to 3000–4000, then polish if needed. If it's still early after that, the long-pin step for a future ball is **80° x 6" x 60°** (VLS ≈ 6" x 5 7/8" x 4 3/8"). Also try a little more speed (16.5–17 mph) and moving left. That changes things more than any layout tweak will.
- **Too late / skids past the breakpoint (unlikely for you on a house shot):** dull the surface toward 2000 first. A stronger layout for a later ball would be about **60° x 5" x 50°** (VLS ≈ 5" x 4 3/8" x 3 3/8").
- **Different family, if you ever want an early, very stable roll:** pin-near-PAP **20° x 2" x 70°** (VLS ≈ 2" x 4 1/8" x 1 7/8"). This option gives the least flare and is predictable in the mid-lane. It works better on medium-heavy oil than on a typical house shot, where your issue is early hook.

## Surface
Start at box finish so you can see the benchmark, but expect to polish it or take it to 3000–4000. A strong asym on a house shot with 430 rpm usually needs a cleaner surface to get down the lane. Surface will move this ball more than layout will. Even with this layout, a strong asym may be **more ball than you need on a fresh house shot**. It may end up being your "fresh/heavier oil" or early-game ball, with a weaker pearl or hybrid as your main house ball.
