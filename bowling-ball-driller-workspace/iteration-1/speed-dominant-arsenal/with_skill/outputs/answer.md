# 4-Ball Sport Arsenal: Lefty, 19.5 mph / 260 rpm

## Bowler profile
- **Hand / style:** left-handed, one-handed (all layouts below are already mirrored for a lefty, so the PAP and pin sit to the **left** of the grip)
- **PAP:** 4 3/4" over x 1 1/4" up
- **Speed:** 19.5 mph, measured at release (no conversion needed)
- **Rev rate:** 260 rpm
- **Axis tilt / rotation:** not given. Your PAP (fairly close to the grip and well above the midline) suggests **above-average tilt**, which is my assumption.
- **Condition:** sport patterns, 41–44 ft
- **Current layout on everything:** 45° x 4 1/2" x 30°, pin up. On your PAP that puts the pin about 5 1/2" above the midline, so it's a length-and-flip layout.

## Read
At 19.5 mph you'd need about **425 rpm** to be matched. At 260 rpm your ratio is **0.61, which is strongly speed-dominant**. In practice the ball gets down the lane before it has time to grip. The high PAP adds tilt, which adds even more length, so you're about one step further toward "too long" than the speed/rev numbers alone suggest.

45° x 4 1/2" x 30° pin-up is a benchmark layout for a *matched* bowler. On you it stacks length on length. That's why everything skids past the breakpoint on 41–44 ft. The layout isn't the only cause, though. Layout is the third lever after **surface** (biggest) and **core/cover strength**. Your arsenal needs rough surfaces and stronger pieces as much as it needs new pin placements.

## Target motion
**Earlier, smoother read in the mid-lane so the ball is already turning when it reaches the breakpoint, not skidding past it.** In practice that means shorter pin-to-PAP (3 1/2"–4", the max-flare zone), lower drilling angles, mostly pin-down, and moderate buffers (about 2") so the motion stays predictable on sport patterns.

PSA-to-PAP for the asymmetric balls depends on each ball's actual pin-to-PSA distance (see below).

## Ball 1: Strong asymmetric (solid)
- **Dual Angle:** 30° x 3 1/2" x 35°, **pin down**
- **VLS / Pin Buffer:** 3 1/2" x PSA-to-PAP x 1 7/8"
  - PSA-to-PAP ≈ **2 3/8"** if the ball's pin-to-PSA is 5", ≈ **3 1/8"** if it's 6". Your driller should use the 30° drilling angle with that ball's real pin-to-PSA, or aim for about 2 1/2"–3" PSA-to-PAP.
- **Where it lands:** pin about 2 7/8" left of grip center and about 1 7/8" **below** the midline (thumb side). PSA ends up fairly close to your PAP.
- **Why:**
  - 3 1/2" pin-to-PAP sits right in the max-flare zone, so the ball revs up and reads the mid-lane.
  - The low 30° drilling angle (PSA near the PAP) makes the ball give up axis rotation sooner, so it hooks earlier and more smoothly.
  - The 35° VAL / 1 7/8" buffer gives a responsive but not jumpy transition.
  - Pin-down favors an earlier, rounder arc.
- **Ball choice matters more than the layout here:** pick a high-differential (≥ 0.050), high int-diff solid with a low-to-medium RG.

## Ball 2: Symmetric benchmark
- **Dual Angle:** drilling angle n/a (symmetric) x **4"** x **35°**, **pin down**
- **VLS / Pin Buffer:** **4" x n/a x 2"** (2" buffer ↔ about 34–35° VAL)
- **Where it lands:** pin about 2 3/4" left of grip center and about 2 3/8" below the midline.
- **Why:**
  - On a symmetric ball, pin-to-PAP and buffer do all the work.
  - 4" gives near-max flare for a strong mid-lane read. The 2" buffer keeps the transition controlled, which is what you want on flat sport patterns where you need to read the ball.
  - This is your reference ball. Every other ball should be clearly earlier or later than this one.
- **Rules:** no balance hole. USBC has banned them since Aug 1, 2020, so drilling angle and PSA don't apply here.

## Ball 3: Pearl asymmetric
- **Dual Angle:** 40° x 4" x 40°, **pin up**
- **VLS / Pin Buffer:** 4" x PSA-to-PAP x 2 3/8"
  - PSA-to-PAP ≈ **2 3/4"** (5" pin-to-PSA) to ≈ **3 3/8"** (6" pin-to-PSA).
- **Where it lands:** pin about 2 3/8" left of grip center and about 4 5/8" **above** the midline.
- **Why:**
  - The pearl cover already provides the length, so the layout doesn't need to add more. That's the mistake in 45 x 4 1/2 x 30 for you.
  - 4" pin keeps the flare high. The 40° drilling angle is a touch earlier than your current 45°.
  - The 40° VAL / 2 3/8" buffer smooths the backend, so it turns rather than skid-flips.
  - Pin up is the only "length" element, which separates this ball from the benchmark.
  - It clears the fronts when the heads dry up and still turns over at the breakpoint.

## Ball 4: Urethane
- **Dual Angle:** n/a x **3 3/4"** x **45°**, **pin down**
- **VLS / Pin Buffer:** **3 3/4" x n/a x 2 1/2"**
- **Where it lands:** pin about 2 1/4" left of grip center and about 1 3/4" below the midline.
- **Why:**
  - Urethane has low flare potential to begin with. At 260 rpm and 19.5 mph you need every bit of read you can get from it, so the pin goes in the 3 1/2"–4" zone.
  - The higher 45° VAL / 2 1/2" buffer keeps the motion smooth and arcing, which is the whole point of urethane.
- **Honest expectation:** on fresh 43–44 ft, urethane will probably go too long for your speed and rev rate. It's your piece for **flat, short-to-medium (41–42 ft) patterns and late-block transition**, or when ball 2 starts hooking too much. Don't expect a layout to make it a heavy-oil ball.
- **Rule check:** confirm your urethane meets the current USBC hardness spec (≥ 73D).

## If it's not quite right
- **Still too long / skidding** (most likely on fresh oil):
  1. Sand the surface down a step first (2000 → 1000 → 500).
  2. Then go to a stronger ball.
  3. Only then consider a stronger layout, e.g. 25° x 3 1/2" x 30° on the asym.
  - Try moving in and slowing down slightly before redrilling.
- **Too early / hooks then rolls out:**
  1. Go one surface step smoother (1000 → 2000 → 3000).
  2. Then move to a weaker step: 45° x 4 1/2" x 40° for the asym, or 4 1/2" x 2 1/2" buffer on the benchmark.

## Surface
- Surface is your biggest lever, and you should **move toward rougher than box** across the arsenal: 500–1000 on the strong solid, 1000–2000 on the benchmark, 2000–3000 on the pearl, 1000–2000 on the urethane.
- Many box finishes are polished or 3000–4000. At 19.5 mph and 260 rpm that adds length you don't need.
- **Before redrilling anything you already own:** take your current balls to 1000–2000 Abralon. That's the cheapest fix for the skidding, and it's reversible. You can't undrill a ball.

## Notes for your driller
- **Lefty:** mirror everything. PAP and pins are on the left side of the grip; the angles and inches don't change.
- **Re-measure the PAP** (4 3/4" x 1 1/4" up) in person before drilling. Every number above depends on it.
- **Pin-to-PSA:** the Storm/VLS PSA-to-PAP values are ranges because they depend on each asymmetric ball's pin-to-PSA (and whether it's marked as PSA or Mass Bias). Use the 30° and 40° drilling angles with the actual ball, or convert from the spec sheet.
- **Pin up/down is stated on purpose:** the same three Dual Angle numbers can be drilled pin above or below the midline. Balls 1, 2 and 4 are pin down; ball 3 is pin up.
- **Pin positions are approximate.** They're from a flattened calculation; mark the exact spots with a layout tool.
- **Flare near the grip:** all four layouts are in the max-flare zone (3 1/2"–4"). At 260 rpm that's intended, not an over-reaction risk. Still, glance at whether the flare rings come near the thumb hole on this PAP.
- **No balance holes** for USBC play.
- **Check the manufacturer's charts:** once you pick specific balls, check each one's layout chart (Storm's layout guide included). These numbers sit on the "earlier / stronger" side of the usual benchmark on purpose, because of the speed-dominance.
